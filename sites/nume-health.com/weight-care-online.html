What is a GLP-1?

GLP-1 receptor agonists are a class of prescription medications that affect appetite signaling and blood-sugar regulation. They are used in diabetes care and, in certain cases, for clinically appropriate weight management.

Outcomes vary. GLP-1 medications are not a quick fix — they're typically used alongside nutrition, activity, and clinician follow-up. A provider will discuss what is appropriate for your medical history.

GLP-1 medications are prescription treatments and are not appropriate for everyone.

5 questions worth asking before starting GLP-1 care

These are good questions for any provider — not just ours.

1. Is the medication FDA-approved or compounded?
Compounded medications are not FDA-approved.
2. What's the all-in monthly cost — including supplies and shipping?
A transparent program tells you the total before you commit. Watch for separate "membership" + "medication" charges that combine into a much higher number.
3. Who is the prescribing entity, and which pharmacy fills the prescription?
Both should be named clearly. If a program won't tell you who is prescribing or dispensing, that's a red flag.
4. What happens if I don't qualify?
A reputable program has a clear refund and "not approved" path. Compounded GLP-1 care is not appropriate for every patient.
5. What ongoing support is included?
Refills, side-effect support, and check-ins should be standard. Ask whether dose changes incur additional fees — they shouldn't.

Safety first

This is a brief overview, not medical advice. Talk to a licensed clinician about your specific history.

Tell your provider about any history of: pancreatitis · medullary thyroid cancer / MEN2 · severe GI disease · gallbladder disease · kidney or liver disease · gastroparesis · bariatric surgery · eating disorders · pregnancy or breastfeeding.
Seek emergency care for severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, signs of an allergic reaction, or any sudden change you're worried about. This program is not for emergencies.
